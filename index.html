01 / RESEARCH QUESTION · DRAFT

How is Spanish used on signs in San Luis Obispo, and what do these signs suggest about their intended audiences?

Our project considers contemporary signage and place naming across selected areas of San Luis Obispo, with proposed comparisons among retail, student-oriented, and industrial or agricultural settings. For signs, we will examine language choice, visibility, and apparent audience. For street names, we will investigate meanings and documented naming histories.

This connects to SPAN 2207 through Spanish in the United States, bilingualism, and the relationship between language and society.

02 / PROPOSED METHODS

Collect. Document. Compare.

  1. Define the area. Choose and record a walking route before collecting signs. Final boundaries are still to be decided.
  2. Document signs. Photograph public-facing Spanish-containing signs consistently along the route. Record exact location, date, wording, languages, and sign type.
  3. Examine street names. Select relevant streets, trace their verified routes, and look for documentary evidence about their names.
  4. Analyze the material. Compare prominence, translation, and purpose. Keep observed details separate from interpretations.
  5. Connect to research. Use academic sources on linguistic landscapes and Spanish in the United States to interpret the evidence.

03 / SCOPE & LIMITS

Defined collection areas, careful comparisons.

The final sample will be limited to the documented collection area and dates. If we collect only Spanish-containing signs, we can describe how Spanish is used but cannot measure its prevalence among all signs.

We will distinguish present-day signs from historical naming practices. Photographs alone cannot establish a business owner’s intentions, the languages of its customers, or a street name’s history.
